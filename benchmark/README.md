# Patient-communications PHI benchmark

Labelled sets of the kind of text clinics send to LLMs: patient SMS, web chat, phone-call transcripts and voicemails, emails, referral letters, fax and intake text. Most open de-identification datasets cover clinical notes; these cover patient communications. Every person and number in them is invented.

**How the blind sets were made.** Each was written by a separate AI agent that had only a brief: the label rules, channels, languages, realistic mess, and a list of things that look like identifiers but are not. It was told not to read the detector code, the tests or the other sets, and it wrote every message from scratch. The v4 to v12 authors also wrote the clinic's own details (`blind/clinic_v4.json` to `blind/clinic_v12.json`) and a 25- or 26-person staff roster (`blind/staff_v4.json` to `blind/staff_v12.json`) before writing any message, as a clinic would configure them. The v6 author wrote small generator scripts with message templates and filled them with different names, numbers and dates, so v6 is less varied than the other sets: 378 of its 400 messages start differently, and 48 messages (12%) are close pairs. The v7 brief then forbade generator scripts; all 400 v7 messages start differently and none are close pairs, though some French and Spanish emails retell English situations. v8 was written by four authors in parallel (English SMS and chat; English calls, email and notes; Spanish and French; Polish, Hebrew and mixed), after a fifth agent wrote the clinic and staff files; none are close pairs. v9 was made the same way from a new brief; none are close pairs. v10 was made the same way from a new brief, after a fifth agent wrote the clinic and staff files; none are close pairs. v11 was made the same way from a new brief, and the fifth agent also wrote a file of staff initials (`blind/staff_initials_v11.json`), so the staff list can tell a staff member's initials from a patient's; none are close pairs. v12 was made the same way from a new brief (a Seattle clinic with a Regina office; family history, legal donor paperwork, travel documents and speech-recognition errors), with four authors writing 100 messages each after a fifth agent wrote the clinic, staff and staff-initials files (`blind/staff_initials_v12.json`); none are close pairs. The sets are independent of the detector code, but they are AI-written, not written by clinic staff.

**Second annotator.** A third agent, also without access to code or detector output, reviewed each blind set against the written label rules and listed missing and wrong labels (`blind/audit_v1.jsonl` to `blind/audit_v12.jsonl`; 45, 149, 2, 50, 6, 11, 62, 16, 47, 63, 68 and 70 corrections; v8 to v12 had two independent annotators per 100 messages and an adjudicator that kept only clearly correct findings, mostly unlabelled repeat mentions of names, staff names, spoken dates and numbers read back by the agent). `blind/convert.py` applies them. All scores below use the corrected labels.

**Frozen scoring.** Before each blind set was scored, the detectors were committed and not changed: v1 against commit 082c2e1, v2 against ab99dc9, v3 against 1dd13f2, v4 against 1f548b0, v5 against b1e794b, v6 against dc33528 (0.1.0a8), v7 against f0c957d (0.1.0a9), v8 against 2a9104e (0.1.0a10), v9 against 3aaa537 (0.1.0a11), v10 against 8db92f4 (0.1.0a12), v11 against 7fb0f01 (main after v10, not yet released), v12 against 650fe26 (0.1.0a13). Those first scores are the fair ones (`results_blind_*_frozen.json`). After a set was scored it was used to find and fix gaps, so its later scores are optimistic. Each set is the re-test of the fixes made on the sets before it. v4 was also scored against 5762fb9, a spoken-date change committed before v4 was seen: it caught one more identifier (88.5% instead of 88.4%) and masked two more non-identifiers, so the spoken-date forms fixed from v1 to v3 were not the ones v4 used.

## Files

- `generate.py`: deterministic generator (fixed seed) for `data/patient_comms_v0.jsonl`.
- `blind/blind_v*.source.jsonl`: the blind sets as written, with inline labels (`[[NAME|jess]]`). `blind/audit_v*.jsonl`: second-annotator corrections. `blind/clinic_v*.json` and `blind/staff_v*.json`: clinic details and staff rosters for `--allow` and `--staff`. `blind/staff_initials_v11.json` and `blind/staff_initials_v12.json`: the initials of the v11 and v12 staff. `blind/convert.py v1|...|v12` builds `data/patient_comms_blind_v*.jsonl`.
- `score.py`: scores every available configuration and prints recall by type, channel and language, precision, and clean-message masking. `--show-misses N`, `--show-false-positives N`, `--allow clinic.json` and `--staff staff.json` help with error analysis. The local-model configurations appear when `phiproxy[gliner]` is installed.
- `results_*.json`: scores for the built-in configurations (v2 to v12 with the clinic details allowed; the frozen first scores with the default policy).

```json
  "id": "sms-00-3",
  "channel": "sms",
  "text": "Hi it's Priya, can I move my bloodwork to Oct 3? My cell is 647-555-0112",
  "spans": [{"start": 8, "end": 13, "type": "NAME"}, ...],
  "known": {"names": ["Priya Natarajan"], "phones": ["647-555-0112"], ...}
}
```

`known` is what the clinic system would already hold about the patient. It lets the benchmark measure the known-entity layer.

## Scoring rules

- A labelled span is **caught** when detected spans cover at least 80% of its characters. The detected type does not have to match, because catching a phone number as an `ID` still protects it.
- Scoring uses the default policy, under which clinician names pass through. Clinician names (`PROVIDER`) are therefore not counted in recall, and masking one counts as a false positive.
- A detected span is **correct** when it overlaps a labelled identifier. **Clean messages masked** is the share of messages without identifiers in which something was masked anyway.
- What counts as an identifier follows HIPAA Safe Harbor (45 CFR 164.514(b)(2)): names of patients and relatives, all date elements except year, ages over 89, geographic units smaller than a state or province (street, city, postal code), contact details, and record, account, plan and other identifying numbers. Workplaces are labelled too because they can identify a person.

## Known limits

- The blind sets are AI-written. Real messages are messier, and a set written by people who work in clinics is still the goal.
- Call transcripts were the weakest channel (86% on v3's first score, 83% on v4's, then 92% on v5's, 97% on v6's, 86% on v10's, 87% on v11's and 91% on v12's): names and numbers spoken in fragments and corrected mid-number, voice-to-text errors ("pre may" for Priya, "for one six" for 416). Faxed notes were 84% on v3's first score and 87% on v4's.
- Hebrew is still the weakest language (77% on v3's first score, 80% on v4's, 86% on v5's, 87% on v7's, 84% on v8's, 85% on v9's, 81% on v10's, 88% on v11's, 90% on v12's; 99% on v6's template-built set): staff and clinic names transliterated into Hebrew ("קיילה", "בלובוניט") are not on the English lists.
- The local model tags insurers, pharmacies and other clinics as a person's workplace ("Gulf Coast Mutual", "Prairie Rx Specialty"). The sets disagree on whether "Dr. X from <clinic>" is a workplace, so these are not filtered.
- Staff named with no cue at all ("Thank you Priya!!") stay masked, so internal staff traffic over-masks even with the staff list.
- Bare days and months tied to a patient ("the twelfth", "until January") are often missed: a bare month is too ambiguous to mask everywhere.
- Still missed without the local model: names with no cue word ("Can Omar come in with me?"), lowercase names that are also words ("joy", "will"). Also missed: workplaces described in a phrase, Hebrew abbreviations of months.
- Very long pasted blobs of letters and dots (thousands of characters, e.g. "A.B.A.B...") take one to two seconds to scan; ordinary 10,000-character messages take about 0.04 s.
- Structured text is read by field name only where the field has a name: Markdown tables, "Name - Maria Lopez" lines and tables without a header row are left to the other rules, and a bare "name" or "first" field counts only beside a surely personal field (or when it holds a full name). Hebrew self-introductions without a cue word ("אני דנה כהן") still need the local model.
- Without a staff list, a staff first name found once is masked again on its later mentions (the echo pass cannot tell it from a patient's).
- French and Spanish bare months after "en" ("en mars") are masked as dates even when they are not a person's date (v10 labels "transfert en mai").
- "Contact Jane at gmail.com" is masked as an email.
- The clinic's own social handle is masked unless it is on the allow list or follows a clear clinic cue ("follow us").
- A clinic address followed by "#N" or "unit N" is masked even when N is the clinic's own unit, unless that form is in the allow list.
- A two-letter code after "patient" or "pt" that is also a common pair of initials ("Pt NS today", "Patient MS and lupus") is masked unless a chart word or number follows.
- Company names after an origin cue ("I'm from Acme Insurance") are masked as a place; the clinic's city in a staff signature block after a sign-off is masked; some capitalised ordinary nouns after Spanish, French or Polish residence verbs are masked.
- The clinic's own street written without its full allow-list entry is masked; staff initials are masked like any initials; clinic closure and policy start dates are masked.
- A house number written after the street name ("Peachtree Park Drive NE 1184") stays partly visible.
- The same phone written in two formats in one message shares one placeholder, so unmasking restores one format.
- A spoken identifier answered more than 3,000 characters after its question on a single line is not tied to the question.
- Unaccented Vietnamese spoken digits are masked only after an unaccented phone cue ("so dien thoai").
- Speech-recognition mishearings of names ("rose marry" for Rosemary) are not caught.
- Links on the clinic's own website are masked, and so are clinic holiday closure dates.
- A lab range written "3.5-12" is masked as a date, and "the 4th floor" is masked as a date.
- An NHS number or a Japanese "My Number" may be labelled `PHONE` (it is masked either way).
- "Father, 100" is read as an age, and the clinic's own city is masked when a patient says they live there.
- UK mobile and Mexican landline numbers without a phone cue are not caught.
- Over-masking costs answer quality, not privacy, but it is real: see the table above.
